# Pilot randomized trial of a smartphone sleep program for night-shift nurses

## Background and design

This report summarizes a single-centre pilot randomized trial of an eight-week smartphone sleep program for night-shift nurses with insomnia symptoms, compared with a waitlist control. The program delivers weekly modules that combine sleep education, stimulus control, a sleep-restriction schedule and relaxation training. Eligible nurses worked at least three night shifts a week and scored 8 or more on the Insomnia Severity Index at screening. Participants were randomized one to one in computer-generated blocks of four, and recruitment took four months. Nurses in the control arm had no contact with the study team before week 8 beyond reminders to complete assessments, and they were offered the program afterwards. The pilot's main purpose was to judge feasibility, so the efficacy figures below are exploratory. All participants were nurses at one teaching hospital, and outcomes were assessed at baseline and at week 8. Participants and assessors were not blinded to allocation.

## Recruitment and retention

Of 112 nurses screened, 64 were randomized, 32 to each arm. Of the 48 nurses who were screened but not randomized, most (31) declined to take part. The two arms were similar at baseline, with mean ages of 36.1 and 36.7 years and mean Insomnia Severity Index scores of 15.2 in the app arm and 14.8 in the control arm. Eight-week retention was 84.4% in the app arm (27 of 32) and 90.6% in the control arm (29 of 32). Across both arms, 56 of 64 participants (85.9%) completed the week 8 assessment. Reasons for not completing were loss to contact (six participants across the two arms) and, in the app arm, time pressure (two participants). Unless stated otherwise, the efficacy figures quoted here are for completers.

## Insomnia severity and sleep time

Insomnia severity was scored from 0 to 28, with lower scores being better. Among completers, the Insomnia Severity Index fell by 5.3 points in the app arm and 1.4 points in the control arm, a between-group difference of 3.9 points (95% CI 1.9 to 5.9). A sensitivity analysis that carried the last observation forward for non-completers gave a smaller but similar difference of 3.2 points (95% CI 1.3 to 5.1). The reduction in insomnia severity persisted after the program ended. The sleep-restriction module is what drove the improvement. Sleep diaries were kept for seven days at each assessment. The sleep-diary estimate of total sleep time was imprecise: the app arm's 16-minute advantage came with a confidence interval running from a 9-minute loss to a 41-minute gain.

## Engagement and safety

Engagement in the app arm was recorded as the number of weekly modules completed. Engagement with the program was high. Adverse events were collected by self-report. No serious adverse events were reported, and the two mild events, both transient worsening of sleep in the first week, occurred in the app arm. These data show the program is safe for night-shift nurses.

## Interpretation

The findings apply to shift workers generally. A definitive trial would need a larger sample and an active comparator.

## Evidence appendix

### T1. Design and procedures

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Single-centre, two-arm, parallel-group pilot randomized trial; open-label (participants and assessors not blinded). |
| Setting | One teaching hospital. Recruitment took four months. |
| Eligibility | Registered nurses working at least 3 night shifts per week; Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) score of 8 or more at screening; no diagnosed sleep disorder. |
| Randomization | 1:1, computer-generated blocks of 4. |
| Intervention | Smartphone app; 8 weekly modules combining sleep education, stimulus control, a sleep-restriction schedule and relaxation training, delivered together in a fixed sequence. |
| Comparator | Waitlist: no contact between baseline and week 8 other than reminders to complete assessments; program offered at week 8. |
| Assessments | Baseline and week 8 only. No assessment after week 8. |
| Measures | ISI (0 to 28, lower is better; self-report). Sleep-diary total sleep time (self-report, 7 days at each assessment). Engagement: number of weekly modules completed (app arm). |
| Efficacy analysis | Completers (participants with a week 8 assessment). Sensitivity analysis with last observation carried forward in all 64 randomized (change = 0 for non-completers). Two-sample comparison of means with 95% confidence intervals. |
| Adverse events | Free-text self-report: at the weekly app check-in (app arm) and at the week 8 assessment (control arm). |
