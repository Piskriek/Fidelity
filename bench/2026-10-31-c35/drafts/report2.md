# What the Wray journal says about the winter of 1741-42 in Hallow Marsh

## Scope of this summary

This note summarizes what Thomas Wray's parish journal says about the hard winter of 1741-42 in Hallow Marsh, and what can reasonably be drawn from it. Wray was the parish clerk, and the manuscript runs to 74 folios. I have worked from the Hallow Marsh Record Society's 1911 transcription and cite it by folio. Each folio reference is to the recto (r) or verso (v) side of a leaf. Where Wray's own words matter I quote them in the appendix rather than paraphrasing at length. I have not tried to reconstruct the parish's finances or population, only to set out what the clerk himself wrote.

## The journal as a record

Wray began the journal on 4 October 1741 and made his last entry on 19 September 1743. The journal contains 212 dated entries, of which 71 concern the weather. After weather, the largest groups are church and vestry business, with 41 entries, and food and prices, with 38. Burials account for 29 entries and poor relief for 17. Entries are fullest in the first half of 1742. Wray kept a continuous day-by-day record of the winter. The 1911 editor judged that several entries were written up some weeks after the dates they carry.

## Grain, the mill and relief

Grain and its price are a recurring subject. Wray gives nine wheat prices in all, the lowest being 4s 6d in October 1741. Wray records wheat at 5 shillings a bushel in November 1741 and at 9 shillings in March 1742. In February 1742 he adds that wheat stood at 7s 6d at Aldby market and that none could be had at the mill. He also notes that Pell's mill stood idle for eleven weeks while its wheel was held fast in the ice, so the parish sent its grain to Aldby for grinding. The vestry responded by laying a rate for the poor, recorded on folio 38v. The surviving folios give a single figure for money raised for poor relief: £11 4s from a rate of fourpence in the pound.

## Burials

Burials rise in the parish register for 1742. The parish register shows 52 burials in 1742, which is 23 more than in 1741. Of the 29 death entries in the journal, Wray names a fever as the cause in seven. The frost alone accounts for the rise in burials in 1742. Wray's burial entries have been checked against the parish register and found accurate. Both the register totals and the journal's own death entries are summarised in the appendix.

## The vicar and the tithe barn

The most pointed passage in the journal concerns the vicar, Mr. Hale. It sits on folio 41r, between the March and April price entries. Wray states that the vicar kept the tithe barn locked against the poor.

## Wider use

The journal gives a representative picture of how rural parishes in the county fared that winter. Folio and page references for every passage are given in the appendix, together with the counts by quarter and by topic.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Text | Thomas Wray, parish clerk of Hallow Marsh: journal kept from 4 October 1741 to 19 September 1743 (716 days counting both end dates). Manuscript of 74 folios. |
| Edition used | Hallow Marsh Record Society transcription, ed. R. Ansell (1911), cited as "Ed." with page numbers. The journal itself is cited by folio, recto (r) or verso (v). |

| Gap in entries | The last entry before the gap is dated 11 December 1741 (fol. 30v) and the next is dated 27 January 1742 (fol. 35r). No entries are dated between 12 December 1741 and 26 January 1742, a period of 46 days. |
| Scope of edition | One parish. The edition prints no records of any other parish and no comparative material (Ed. p. viii). |

### E6. Passages (journal by folio, edition by page)

- Q1, fol. 36r: "Wheat 7s 6d the bushel at Aldby market, and none to be had at the mill."
- Q2, fol. 37v: "Old Hannah Voss was taken by the cold on the Tuesday night, and buried on the Friday."
- Q3, fol. 38v: "The vestry laid a rate of fourpence in the pound for the poor, and it brought in eleven pounds four shillings."
- Q4, fol. 39v: "Pell's mill stood idle eleven weeks, the wheel being bound fast in the ice, and the parish sent its grain to Aldby for grinding."
- Q5, fol. 41r: "It is said by some that our vicar, Mr. Hale, keeps the tithe barn locked against the poor. I have not seen it with my own eyes, and Mr. Hale gives out that the barn is empty."
- Q6, fol. 52r: "Three of the Marsh Lane children are down with the fever, and it is said to be abroad in the parish."
- Q7, Ed. p. xv: "Several entries are plainly written up from notes some weeks after the day they bear, and Wray's dating must be used with caution."
- Q8, Ed. p. xxii: "I have compared nine of Wray's burial notices with the register and find all nine entered there on the same or an adjacent day. The remainder I have not had leisure to check."
