## Energy gateway

### 1. Mechanism and rules

- **Conservation.** Energy is conserved at every level. Stars are the primary source of ordinary physics. (Stars follow Ra 41.4. Conservation is our rule; Ra is silent on it.)
- **Vortex core.** Two counter-spun toroids relax toward a minimum-energy state while conserving magnetic helicity (Taylor relaxation). Relaxation converts stored magnetic energy and cannot raise it. The core is never a source.
- **Gateway.** A toroid couples to its enclosing level through a declared transfer term. It moves energy from that level into the toroid and creates none.
- **Access.** A gateway opens only above the entity's balance threshold (follows Ra 29.27, 29.29). Ra gives no units, so the threshold is a design parameter.
- **Logging.** Every draw is logged with source level, amount and step. Overdraw sets a distortion flag (consequence in kind follows Ra 22.23, 24.4).

### 2. Physical basis

Fidelity's vortex core rests on Taylor relaxation, a plasma-physics result established since 1974. A twisted magnetic field conserves magnetic helicity and relaxes into a minimum-energy toroidal state. In our idealised axisymmetric model, two counter-spun toroids release 33% to 96% of their stored magnetic energy as they relax, depending on imbalance, and a closed-form approximation matches within 0.2 percentage points. The toroid converts stored energy and never creates it. The gateway is a specified transfer rule: energy enters a level only through it, is debited from the enclosing level, and is logged, so every run balances.

### 3. Default parameters

- Grid: 40 × 40 (reference); 20 × 20 for quick runs only.
- Starts: two counter-spun toroids, imbalance b = 0 (balanced). Reference release 32.9%.
- Validation targets: relaxed λ_eff ≈ 4.955; magnetic axis at r = 0.625.
- Gateway threshold: 0.50 on a 0–1 balance scale (design).
- Draw cap: 1% of the enclosing level's energy per step (design).
- Transfer loss: 10%, logged as dissipation (design; Ra names no cost).
- Level floor: gateway closes below 10% of the enclosing level's initial energy (design).
