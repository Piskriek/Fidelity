## The fourth place

**CHOICE:** a framing or definitional choice the stated relation depends on, a decision someone makes that a different chooser could make differently. Examples are the number system or ring, the universe, the units, the encoding, the reference frame, the measurement precision, the bit width, and the identification of objects. Two rules apply:
- **The relation words keep their standard meaning.** Redefining "coprime" or "disjoint" is not a choice. It changes the question.
- **The alternative must be one actually used in practice.** A real number system, unit convention, encoding or frame counts; an invented one does not.

## Grades

- **SENSITIVE:** there is a real alternative choice under which A and B (as described) still make sense, and the stated relation fails or changes.
- **INVARIANT:** a real choice is involved, but the relation holds under every real alternative, so it is an invariant.
- **NONE:** no real choice is involved, the candidate is false, or it only redefines the relation words, renames things, or is irrelevant.

A pair has a fourth element if a majority of the three judges grades any candidate SENSITIVE or INVARIANT.

## Method

- **Proposers.** Opus, Sonnet and Haiku each propose, for every pair: their strongest choice candidate, the real alternative choice, and what happens to the relation under it, plus their nearest miss.
- **Judges.** Opus, Sonnet and Haiku grade every candidate blind. They do not see this file, which pairs are controls, or the hypotheses.
- **Planted decoys.** Four candidates that should be graded NONE are mixed in at fixed positions:
  - D1: redefining "coprime" to mean "both odd";
  - D2: the font of the braces;
  - D3: whether the listener likes the sound;
  - D4: calling the positron "electron 2".

## Limits

- All proposers and judges are Claude models from one vendor.
- One run, with no repeats.
- "Used in practice" is a judgment call, and the judges make it.
- The decoys test only obvious leniency. Subtle over-crediting would pass them.
- An INVARIANT grade asserts a universal ("holds under every real alternative"), which the judges can only check against the alternatives they think of.
